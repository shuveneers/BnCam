## 1. Ausgangsqualität

The active physical chroma estimator used short bilateral supports in camera RGB, before WB/CCM. Its centre-versus-neighbour rejection left visible fine coloured speckles, particularly in dark neutral B surfaces. Spectra already propagated the physical RGB covariance through WB/CCM for diagnostics; the pixel estimator did not operate in that amplified domain. The previous two weight-strengthening attempts remain reverted.

The fixed lab set is nine independent main-camera RAW10 inputs (A1–3, B1–3, D1–3), with their original frame-specific DNG lens-shading map, black/white levels, WB, CCM, AF hints and rotation. RAW and DNG SHA-256 hashes were checked before replay. Every comparison uses the same RAW/context on both sides, three actual algorithms, two renders per algorithm. This is a causal OLD/NEW experiment, not a claim that native replay is bit-identical to the historical camera publication.

## 2. Candidate 1 — KEEP

Replace the existing physical chroma estimator with a 5×5 local covariance Wiener estimator. The exact linear transform is `T = CCM × diag(WB)`. The existing Spectra covariance helper computes `Σout = T Σin Tᵀ`. Samples and covariance are analysed in this same domain, in Y/R−G/B−G coordinates. Chroma is whitened with its correlated 2×2 covariance; local covariance eigen-directions decide which residual can be explained by noise. Strong colour structure releases to identity. Luma-derived neighbour support prevents averaging across strong luminance boundaries; the signal/noise ratio limits authority at good SNR. All output positions remain full-resolution 4:4:4.

Only the chroma residual is corrected; analysis-domain Y is unchanged within float32 tolerance. The correction is transported through `T⁻¹` back into the established fused colour pass, so production WB/CCM still runs exactly once. No saturation control, sharpening, RGB blur, extra full-frame image or denoiser was added. Missing physical evidence bypasses filtering; a singular colour transform retains the camera-domain estimator.

The existing luma filter's pixel algorithm is unchanged. Its CPU fallback now reads the same immutable demosaic as the GPU and adds its existing correction to post-chroma RGB; this is necessary because camera-domain Y need not equal WB/CCM-domain Y. A nonidentity WB/CCM test checks both chroma-only Y identity and combined chroma/luma CPU/GPU parity, including signed and HDR samples.

The first complete 27-pair comparison shows visibly cleaner dark neutral B crops, with petal veins/edges and colour boundaries retained. D paper/text backgrounds contain fewer colour speckles, without clearer letters being manufactured by sharpening. A retains colour, highlight appearance and local contrast. Final numbers below are populated from the final build, not from an automatic metric winner.

## Evidence and practical limits

Large replay/crop/build/device artefacts are in ignored `work/quality-run/`. JSON metrics are JPEG-domain proxies, not calibrated sensor-noise or ground-truth detail scores. Decoded JPEG endpoints can change through DCT/quantisation even when the exact pre-JPEG clipping telemetry is zero. No fabricated edge-width/halo score: the physical crops lack a certified isolated straight edge. Synthetic clean colour edges, lines, textile and text cases exercise these safety properties separately. Full-frame nonfinite counters cover demosaic input and post-colour output, not every intermediate tone buffer.
